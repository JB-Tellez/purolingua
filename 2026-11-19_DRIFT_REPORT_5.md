## src/components/README.md

- **[FIXED 2026-11-19: the `role="status"` region is always mounted and only its text changes; tests in `FeedbackMessage.test.tsx`; checked in Chrome (same element, "Sbagliato", then empty). Not checked with a screen reader.]** **Feedback may not be announced: the live region appears together with its message.** `FeedbackMessage` returns `null` when `state` is `null` and renders `<p role="status" aria-live="polite">` only with a message. Screen readers watch live regions that already exist; a region inserted with its text already inside is often not announced (behaviour varies by browser and screen reader). The Rephrase session contract states the feedback is "visible and announced once whichever side is up", and the components contract only says it "renders nothing when `state === null`". Keeping the `role="status"` element mounted and changing only its text would make announcements reliable. Not checked with a screen reader.
- **[FIXED 2026-11-19: each is labelled `study.audioChoice` ("Ascolta: {text}" / "Escuchar: {text}"); tests in `ChoiceButton`, `a11yLabels`, and `badgeMessages.test.ts`; checked in Chrome.]** **The four speak buttons beside the answer choices share one name.** Every `ChoiceButton`'s speak button is labelled `study.audio` ("Ascolta" / "Escuchar"), as is `AudioButton`. On a question, a screen reader user hears the same name four times, with only DOM order (each sits just before its answer button) to tell which answer it plays. The contract documents the label but not the ambiguity. Including the choice text in the name (for example "Ascolta: …") would fix it.

## src/data/qa/README.md

- **[FIXED 2026-11-20 (the user chose to lengthen foils, not correct answers): one foil on each of 22 cards was extended in Italian and Spanish together, so the correct answer is now strictly the longest on 15/56 Italian and 14/56 Spanish cards (about chance). Card ids and correct answers are unchanged, so stored progress is unaffected. `src/__tests__/data/qaChoices.test.ts` caps it at a third per language and checks that the four choices differ after `normalize`. Most foils are still off-topic (e.g. train times in the café), which can also give the answer away; that was left as is.]** **The correct answer is usually the longest choice.** In 65 of the 112 language/card combinations (58%), the correct answer is strictly longer than all three foils; with four options and no length bias that would be about 25%. A learner can often pick the right answer without understanding it. This is content, not code: shortening some correct answers or lengthening some foils would fix it, and the scan could become a data test. The contract describes the foils' shape (fixed 3-tuples) but sets no rule for them.
